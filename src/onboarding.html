GMRec · getting started

Record each person in a call as their own file

GMRec takes every participant's video straight from the live stream the meeting delivers, not from pixels on your screen. Each camera and screen share becomes its own clean file, at the ratio their camera actually sends. It knows Google Meet and ADPList out of the box, and you can add any other meeting site yourself.

Before anything else: tell everyone in the meeting that you are recording, and record only with their agreement. Recording people without consent is illegal in many places. Nothing GMRec does is hidden from you, but nothing tells the other participants either.

  1. Check your camera and microphone

    Open Device setup once and allow access. This picks which camera and microphone go into your own file, and confirms Chrome can reach them. Nothing is recorded during the test.

    Skip this if you only want to record other people.

  2. Open your meeting and pick who to record

    In the meeting tab, click the GMRec toolbar icon. The Record tab lists every camera and screen share it can see, updating as people join, leave or start presenting. Tick the ones you want. Each tick is a separate file.

  3. Start, and leave it alone

    Press Start recording — in the popup, or on the control bar GMRec puts on the meeting page itself. Resizing the window, switching tabs, covering a tile with a menu or scrolling it out of view changes nothing: the recording does not come from the screen.

    If someone starts sharing their screen mid-recording, a prompt asks whether to add it as another file.

  4. Stop, and find your folder

    Everything from one session lands in a single folder named for the meeting and its date. Inside, files carry the participants' names, plus meeting-audio.m4a — a standalone recording of the whole meeting's sound — and recording-info.json, which describes what each file contains.

    Choose where that folder goes under Setup → Save location.

Worth knowing

It keeps going

A camera switching off, a voice dropping, a participant leaving, even the meeting ending — none of it finalises a file. Recording continues until you press Stop, so one timeline covers the session.

Nothing leaves this device

Files are written to your own Downloads folder. Chunks are kept in the extension's local storage as a backup while recording, and you can clear them from the Files tab. There is no server and no account.

Quality is the meeting's ceiling

GMRec records exactly what the call sends and re-encodes it as H.264 High profile MP4. On Google Meet it pins a tile you select, because Meet sends more detail for a prominent tile — but nothing can add detail that was never sent.

Whose voice goes where

Your microphone goes only into your own file. A participant's file carries their own voice when the site exposes it separately, and the meeting's shared audio when it does not.
